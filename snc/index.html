INSIGHTS
▲
Month-16 Team Cliff: 150M Tokens, a 333% Supply Shock in a Single Event: circulating supply jumps from 45M to 195M in one cliff expiry, the most dangerous event in the schedule
▾
The team and advisor allocation (15% = 150,000,000 tokens) is subject to a 16-month full lockup followed by 24-month linear vesting. The cliff expiry at month 16 post-TGE is structurally the most dangerous event in the entire unlock schedule. At that point, circulating supply leaps from 45,000,000 to 195,000,000, a 333% increase in effective supply relative to the pre-cliff float. At the listing price of $0.02, this represents $3,000,000 in potential team sell pressure entering a market that had only $450,000 in initial market cap and $50,000 in provisioned liquidity. Even if only 10% of team tokens are sold at market, that is $300,000 of sell pressure against what is likely still a thin order book. The market maker's own spreadsheet explicitly flags this as a "CRITICAL" risk period. Any trader aware of this schedule will begin positioning short from month 14 onward, creating a self-fulfilling sell-side dynamic before the cliff formally expires.
▲
$50K Liquidity Against a $450K MCAP Is Critically Thin: a single $10K sell order can move price by 20%; the market maker strategy relies on volume it hasn't generated
▾
The market maker strategy document specifies $50,000 USDT as the initial liquidity alongside 2.5 million SNC tokens at the TGE price of $0.02. Against an initial market cap of $450,000, the liquidity ratio is approximately 11%, low even by micro-cap standards. The document targets $250,000 in average daily volume in month 1, which is 5x the total liquidity provisioned. Generating that volume without genuine organic demand would require artificial wash trading, which itself is a market manipulation risk. The order book depth target of $50,000 at ±2% is insufficient to absorb any meaningful institutional or OTC buyer entering the market. A single whale-sized $10,000 sell order would move price by approximately 20% at this depth. The buyback safety wall at $0.018 provides some floor protection but only if the issuer maintains it continuously, which is operationally dependent on treasury funding.
▲
Staking Module Exists on Paper But Has No Defined Economics: No APY, No Pool Size, No Emission Rate: the staking contract is described in the whitepaper but zero quantitative parameters are provided
▾
The whitepaper describes a Staking Module that "allows users to deposit tokens in exchange for a reward" and explicitly positions staking as a mechanism to absorb token supply and reduce sell pressure. This is directly relevant to the M16 team cliff risk, since staking lockup is one of the few mechanisms that could keep tokens off the market during high-inflation periods. However, not a single quantitative parameter is disclosed: no APY range, no total staking reward pool size, no emission rate for staking rewards, no lock-up period required to earn rewards, and no minimum stake threshold. The whitepaper only adds that "the reward system does not use excessive privileges" and is "lean." This is insufficient for any investor to model staking participation rates or assess how much of the circulating supply the staking module can realistically absorb. Without these parameters, staking cannot be underwritten as a genuine deflationary or sell-pressure-reduction mechanism. It is a feature that exists in the smart contract code but has no published economic model behind it.
▲
Token Utility Structure Generates No Independent Buy Pressure: Payment Gateway Requires Existing Users: SNC's current utility set-up is entirely contingent on pre-existing customers, creating a circular demand problem
▾
The core utility of SNC is described as a universal payment token across eight branded verticals: FLY, PAY, CARS, ESTATE, YACHTS, DATA, HEALTH, and DEV. This is a legitimate utility category, but the economic structure of this model creates a fundamental buy-pressure problem. For SNC to generate organic token demand, a customer must first become a Scandic ecosystem client, then choose to pay in SNC rather than fiat, then acquire SNC from the market to do so. Each step in this chain is a conversion drop-off. The customer base for Scandic's services is a luxury clientele that is primarily fiat-native and has no structural reason to acquire SNC ahead of a transaction. There is no speculative demand mechanism that pulls in buyers independent of service consumption: no yield-bearing staking (APY rates are entirely undisclosed), no protocol fee buyback into the token, no revenue-funded treasury with governance rights over yield distribution. The governance function is described as "in the planning stage but not activated at the time of issuance." In tokenomics terms, SNC currently has consumption utility but not investment utility, meaning buy demand can only come from service users, not from investors seeking yield or governance leverage. This is a structurally weak demand foundation for a token facing 333% supply expansion at M16.
■
2.25% TGE Float Is Below the Optimal Range and Opens Manipulation Risk: 22.5M tokens at $0.02 = $450K MCAP, far too small for genuine price discovery
▾
The TGE circulating supply of 22,500,000 tokens (2.25% of total) is below the 3-5% floor considered minimal for legitimate price discovery. While a low float can protect against early dump pressure, at this extreme it creates two distinct risks. First, with only $450,000 in market cap at listing, any coordinated buyer can establish an artificial price level with minimal capital, typical of a pump-and-dump setup. Second, the subsequent unlock schedule (slow from M0-M12, then explosive at M16) means buyers at TGE who see the price rise are unaware of the coming 333% supply expansion at month 16. The market maker strategy document appears to be designed to manage this dynamic by establishing upward price momentum ($0.05 target, $0.10 bullish target) before the team cliff hits, which would allow early participants to exit at a profit while later entrants absorb the dilution.
■
Year-1 Inflation 100% From M0 to M12, Then 333% at M16: supply doubles in the first year, then nearly quintuples at the team cliff, sustained pressure with no absorption mechanism
▾
The unlock schedule produces two distinct inflation waves. The first runs from M0 to M12, during which circulating supply doubles from 22.5M to 45M (100% increase) as private investor tranches and marketing allocations vest incrementally. This is manageable if protocol adoption grows in parallel. The second wave is the M16 cliff event (150M team tokens), which expands circulating supply by 333% from 45M to 195M in a single scheduled event. There is no disclosed mechanism to absorb this supply, no buyback program, no burn schedule tied to revenue, and no staking lock-up that would keep tokens off market. The whitepaper mentions a staking module in the smart contract architecture, but provides no APY rates, staking ratios, or commitment that staking will attract sufficient token lockup to offset the cliff pressure. By month 24, 350M tokens (35%) are in circulation, with the trajectory continuing upward through the full vesting schedule.
✓
CertiK Audit and KYC Verification Completed: Smart Contract Security Confirmed: formal verification, no critical/major issues unresolved, team KYC with quarterly re-assessment
▾
The whitepaper confirms that Scandic Coin underwent a CertiK security audit covering both on-chain smart contract code and off-chain corporate structure. CertiK's Skynet report shows no critical or major issues remaining unresolved, and the residual findings have been addressed. The team also completed CertiK's KYC/AML program, which includes video interview with founders, identity and document verification, and OSINT background review. CertiK is a credible and widely-recognized auditor in the blockchain space. The quarterly reassessment of the KYC badge provides ongoing accountability. This is a genuine positive that reduces smart contract exploit risk and establishes baseline team accountability. However, it is important to note that a CertiK audit validates code correctness, not tokenomics design, the structural risks identified in this audit (supply concentration, cliff events, issuer control) are design choices, not code vulnerabilities.
✓
$20M FDV Conservative for a Multi-Brand RWA Ecosystem With 30-Year Operating History: physical assets (jets, yachts, real estate), MiCA/VARA compliance, and 8 active ecosystem verticals
▾
At $0.02 per token with 1B total supply, the listing FDV is $20M. For a project claiming 30 years of operating experience across luxury travel, finance, real estate, and mobility verticals, and with physical assets including jets and yachts, this FDV is not obviously inflated. Comparable RWA tokenization projects in the luxury segment have launched at FDVs of $30-100M. The stated compliance with both EU MiCA regulation and Dubai VARA standards, combined with the CertiK audit, establishes a more credible regulatory posture than most projects at this stage. The eight branded business lines (FLY, PAY, CARS, ESTATE, YACHTS, DATA, HEALTH, DEV) provide genuine breadth of utility vectors if even a fraction are operationally deployed to accept SNC. The planned Scandic Pay Wallet and Scandic Crypto Card for Q1 2026 represent concrete product milestones. However, none of the stated revenues or physical asset valuations are independently verified in the whitepaper, and "30 years of experience" in the legacy business does not automatically translate to Web3 product-market fit.
