INSIGHTS
✓
1.35% TGE Float is Among the Lowest of Any Token Audited: only 2.7M tokens in circulation on day one, IMC $541K
▾
Only 2,707,500 $YLD tokens are in circulation at TGE, 1.35% of total supply, producing an initial market cap of approximately $541,500. This is an exceptionally conservative TGE float, well below typical DeFi launches (which often start at 10-20% float). The practical effect is minimal immediate sell pressure: total estimated TGE day-one selling across all allocations is approximately $252K, which is manageable against even thin DEX liquidity of $200K from the Liquidity allocation. The ultra-low float means price is highly sensitive to demand signals in the first weeks after listing, which cuts both ways: positive catalysts (plantation yield payouts, staking rewards going live) can drive sharp appreciation, but any concentrated sell can also move price significantly. The Treasury cliff of 24 months and the 72-month vesting tail are the structural backstop that keeps this float tight through the protocol's critical early growth phase.
✓
Treasury Structure Demonstrates Long-Term Alignment: 37.75% locked 24 months then vested 72 months
▾
The Treasury holds 75.5M tokens (37.75% of total supply) with a 24-month cliff and 72-month linear vesting, meaning no Treasury tokens enter circulation until 2028 at the earliest (assuming H1 2026 TGE), and the full Treasury isn't distributed until 2034. This is an unusually long lock-up and sends a strong signal about the founders' long-term orientation. Combined with an annual $YLD buyback program funded by 5% of $HECTARE sales and 5% of plantation revenues (targeting $20.88M in cumulative buybacks by 2033), the Treasury structure creates a flywheel: revenue compounds into buybacks, buybacks reduce circulating supply, reduced supply amplifies yield per $YLD holder. The DAO Fund (3%, 12m cliff, 36m vest) adds further governance-controlled reserve flexibility without concentrating power in founders.
✓
Dual-Token Model With Genuine Separation of Functions: $HECTARE (security) pays USDT yield; $YLD (utility) unlocks services
▾
The two-token architecture is genuinely well-designed for an RWA project. $HECTARE functions as a security token: it represents a digitized share of plantation profits, pays yield in stablecoins, requires KYC/AML, and has contractually guaranteed minimum yields with Swiss legal backing. $YLD is the utility layer: it cannot claim plantation yield without being burned through the PoL mechanism, gates the secondary market, powers staking, and enables governance. The mandatory $YLD removal required to claim USDT income from plantations creates persistent, non-speculative demand for $YLD regardless of market conditions. This is a textbook token utility design: holders cannot simply hold $HECTARE for passive yield without actively engaging the $YLD ecosystem. Competitor projects (Treecoin, Dimitra, KlimaDAO) lack this yield-access gating mechanism.
■
"Up to 30% Annual Yield" Claim Requires Independent Verification: Paulownia trees planted 2023, first meaningful harvest 2030
▾
The headline "up to 30% annual yield" is the central commercial claim, but it rests on a Paulownia-specific assumption that is difficult to verify independently at this stage. Paulownia reaches harvestable maturity in approximately 7 years from planting, meaning the 150,000 trees planted in 2023 at the Fergana site won't yield meaningful timber income until approximately 2030. The interim yield ($3M in sales so far, described as 450 private investors) appears to derive primarily from ongoing asset sales rather than plantation revenue. The lightpaper mentions intensive gardens and GDS Mining (12.5 MW hydro-powered) as supplementary income streams during the tree growth phase, which is a legitimate risk-mitigation approach, but neither is independently audited. The LightPaper explicitly states that minimum yield is contractually guaranteed and financial details are confidential (NDA required). Until an independent auditor certifies the plantation operations and yield projections, the 30% headline should be treated as a best-case projection rather than a validated return.
■
Advisory Vesting (6 Months) Is Too Short for a Long-Horizon RWA Project: full 6M tokens vested by month 18, then advisors have no lock-in
▾
Advisors hold 6M $YLD (3%) with a 12-month cliff followed by only 6-month linear vesting. This means all advisory tokens are fully vested and unlocked by month 18 post-TGE. For a project with a 7-year Paulownia plantation cycle, an 8-year Treasury schedule, and a $1B long-term growth target by 2027, 18 months of advisor retention incentive is misaligned. By contrast, the Team has a 12-month cliff with 36-month vesting (48 months total), which is appropriate. Advisors with early access to strategic information can exit fully before the protocol's critical growth phases (Launchpad launch in H2 2026, secondary market opening, first material plantation yields). This creates a governance risk: advisors who have fully vested have reduced incentive to support long-term protocol health. Extending advisory vesting to 24-36 months would better align incentives with the plantation timeline.
■
$2M Strategic Round at $20M FDV Implies 2x at TGE: Strategic investors at $0.10 enter at 2x at listing FDV of $40M
▾
Strategic investors are entering at $0.10 per token against a $40M listing FDV, giving them a 2.0x paper multiple at TGE. Seed investors at $0.055 hold a 3.6x multiple. NFT round at $0.085 holds 2.4x. These are notably compressed compared to typical DeFi rounds, where 10x to 50x investor multiples are common. However, there are specific structural concerns worth flagging. Strategic investors face a 9-month cliff and then only 12 months of linear vesting, their first unlock at month 10 begins when the protocol is still in early growth phase. The 9-month cliff aligns closely with when the PoL protocol should be active and plantation yields beginning to flow. Strategic investors who are dissatisfied with protocol trajectory have a relatively short window to exit before advisors. The total fundraising of $2.967M raised (with $2.4M remaining to raise) against a $40M TGE FDV means the protocol is relatively lean on runway, and the $2M Strategic round is critical to reaching TGE and listing milestones.
■
Retrodrop 3.5% Has No Defined Vesting Schedule: 7M tokens with no cliff, no vest timetable disclosed
▾
The Retrodrop allocation (7M tokens, 3.5% of supply) shows no cliff and no vesting period in the Excel model, the schedule field reads as a dash. The LightPaper describes retrodrops as "rewards for staking participants" but provides no on-chain smart contract schedule or time-lock for distribution. In the emissions model, these tokens contribute zero circulating supply across the modeled 96 months, suggesting they are held in reserve and distributed at the project team's discretion. This discretionary control is a governance risk: unannounced retrodrop distributions could create unexpected sell pressure on the open market and dilute existing staking reward expectations. The Airdrop allocation (1M, 50% at TGE) is clearly defined and modest. The Retrodrop needs an explicit on-chain vesting schedule before TGE to remove team discretion over these tokens.
■
Uzbekistan Operational Risk Is Under-Disclosed in Token Docs: plantation lease in Fergana, legal entity in Switzerland, token issuer in Singapore
▾
The three-jurisdiction structure (Swiss entity, Singapore token issuer, Uzbekistan plantation) introduces meaningful operational and regulatory risk that is largely absent from the tokenomics documentation. The Fergana Valley is a productive agricultural region, but Uzbekistan's land lease framework for foreign operators has changed significantly since 2017 economic reforms. Force majeure events (natural disaster, political instability, commodity price collapse for Paulownia timber) are not disclosed as risk factors in the deck or LightPaper. The LightPaper states that "minimum yield and collateral obligations are contractually binding" but the underlying contract (between the Swiss entity and the Uzbek leaseholders) is confidential and shared only under NDA. Token holders cannot independently verify the contractual protection without signing the NDA, creating a transparency gap that is fundamental for an asset that markets itself on "on-chain transparency." An independent plantation audit report should be published before TGE.
▲
Year-1 Inflation of 912%: The Low TGE Float Masks Extreme Unlock Velocity: circulating supply 10x from 2.7M to 32.5M within 12 months
▾
The 1.35% TGE float is not a sign of structural conservatism, it is a direct consequence of every allocation having a cliff. Once those cliffs expire simultaneously in the first year, the unlock cascade is severe. Circulating supply goes from 2.71M at TGE to 32.46M by month 12: a 912% increase in 12 months. Month 7 is the single worst calendar event: Strategic (9-month cliff), Marketing (6-month cliff), and Liquidity (6-month cliff) all begin vesting in the same window, adding 2.9M new tokens in a single month and pushing month-on-month inflation to 28%. Month 10 adds another 4.6M tokens when Public (6-month cliff) and NFT (1-month cliff) vesting accelerates. By month 13, Advisory and Team begin unlocking simultaneously on top of all the ongoing schedules, adding 6M additional tokens per month through month 15. Year 2 continues the dilution: supply grows another 170% to 87.7M (43.8% of total supply) by month 24. In practical terms, for the buyback program to neutralise this, it would need to absorb the sell pressure from 29.3M new tokens entering year-1 circulation alone. At $0.20 listing price that is $5.86M of potential sell-side supply, against a projected $1M in year-1 buybacks. Even under the optimistic scenario the buyback covers roughly 17% of new supply in year 1. Inflation only normalises from year 3 onward (39% YoY in year 3, 17% in year 4, stabilising around 7-8% from year 5). The project's price resilience in the first 18 months will depend almost entirely on whether $HECTARE sales generate enough real demand to absorb this supply expansion.
▲
$YLD Buyback Model Depends Entirely on $HECTARE Sales Growth: $1M buyback in 2026, $20.88M by 2033 requires exponential sales growth
▾
The $YLD buyback program is the primary deflationary mechanism and price support for $YLD holders, funded by 5% of $HECTARE sales and 5% of plantation revenues. The modeled buyback schedule shows $1M in 2026 growing to $20.88M cumulative by 2033. To generate $20.88M in cumulative buybacks through these two 5% sources, the protocol needs approximately $208M in combined $HECTARE sales and plantation revenue over 8 years. The roadmap targets $27.5M in sales by H2 2026 and $1B by 2027, which is highly ambitious. $3M in current sales across 450 investors is a solid pre-TGE proof point, but the jump from $3M to $27.5M in 12 months and then $1B by year two is a non-linear growth curve that would require a significant marketing and expansion execution that is not yet demonstrated. If sales growth underperforms by 50%, the buyback program halves, the deflationary model weakens, and $YLD staker yields are directly compressed. The $YLD value proposition is structurally dependent on the underlying $HECTARE sales machine scaling as projected.
